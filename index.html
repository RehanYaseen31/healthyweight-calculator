How this calculator works

  1. We convert your height to metres. Centimetres are divided by 100; feet and inches are converted at 1 in = 2.54 cm.
  2. We apply the healthy adult BMI band. Health organisations link the lowest risk to a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9, so your range is 18.5 × height² up to 24.9 × height².
  3. If you add your weight, we compute your BMI and place it in a category — but only for ages 18 and over, because adult cut-offs don't fit younger bodies.

The BMI formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

In imperial units: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)² — the 703 simply converts the units so the result matches the metric formula.

Worked example

A person is 170 cm tall (1.70 m) and weighs 70 kg:

  • Healthy range: 18.5 × 1.70² = 53.5 kg to 24.9 × 1.70² = 72.0 kg.
  • BMI: 70 ÷ 1.70² = 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2 — inside the healthy band.
  • Difference: 70 kg sits within the 53.5–72.0 kg range, so no gap to report.

These formulas give estimates, not a universal medical target — two people at the same height can have different healthy weights depending on build, muscle and health history.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy weight range?

It's the span of body weights linked to the lowest health risk for your height. This calculator estimates it from the adult BMI band of 18.5–24.9: the lower bound is 18.5 × height² and the upper bound is 24.9 × height² (height in metres, result in kilograms).

How should I measure my height?

Stand straight against a wall without shoes, heels together, and mark the top of your head. Measure in centimetres, or in feet and inches. Measure in the morning if possible — height can vary slightly through the day.

Should I use kilograms or pounds?

Either. Use the unit toggle above the form to switch between metric (kg, cm) and imperial (lb, ft/in). The calculator converts internally and always shows results in both units.

Why does the calculator ask for my age?

Adult BMI categories don't apply to children and teenagers, whose healthy ranges depend on age- and sex-specific growth charts. If you're under 18, we show an age-appropriate notice and skip the adult categories.

Are ideal weight formulas accurate?

They're estimates, not medical targets. BMI-based ranges don't account for muscle mass, bone density, body composition, pregnancy or medical conditions. Use the result as a starting point and discuss personal targets with a qualified health professional.

What if my weight is outside the healthy range?

Treat it as information, not a verdict. Small, sustainable changes to activity, sleep and nutrition matter more than chasing a single number. If you have concerns, speak with a doctor or registered dietitian rather than acting on a calculator alone.
